# A Comparative Study of Classical and Quantum Support Vector Machines for Multimodal Oral Cancer Detection

**Authors:** Nipun Joshi, Sanchit Baweja, Sandeep Kumar, Vikrant Shokeen, Amit Sharma, Vijeet Gahlawat  
**Venue:** IEEE International Conference on Data Mining Workshops (ICDMW), 2025  
**Pages:** 493–499  
**DOI:** 10.1109/ICDMW69685.2025.00062  
**Bibliographic record:** https://dblp.org/rec/conf/icdm/JoshiBKSSG25

## Why this paper is especially valuable

Rather than presenting a quantum classifier alone, the paper explicitly frames the research as a **classical SVM vs quantum SVM comparison** for multimodal oral-cancer detection.

That is exactly the experimental philosophy a credible student project should follow.

## Research theme

The central question is whether quantum support-vector-machine methods provide useful behavior when oral-cancer information comes from multiple modalities.

## Why multimodal matters

Real cancer assessment is rarely based on one isolated numerical feature. A multimodal research system could potentially combine representations from sources such as:

- clinical/intraoral imagery
- histopathology
- demographic/clinical variables
- molecular data, where available

Your implementation should only combine modalities supported by the dataset you actually obtain.

## Suggested extension

Compare:

1. Linear SVM
2. RBF SVM
3. Random Forest / XGBoost
4. Neural network
5. Quantum kernel SVM / QSVM

Then study whether any observed difference remains after controlling for:
- dimensionality reduction
- identical train/test splits
- hyperparameter tuning
- class imbalance
- repeated cross-validation
