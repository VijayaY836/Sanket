## Why include a non-quantum paper?

A QML project is scientifically weak if it compares itself only with weak baselines.

This recent OSCC paper gives your team a view of what **modern classical ML/deep learning** can already do on histopathological oral-cancer tasks.

## Research problem

Automated detection of Oral Squamous Cell Carcinoma from histopathological imagery.

## Project use

Treat papers like this as the benchmark your quantum model must compete against.

A good experiment asks:

> Given the same data and feature budget, does the quantum component offer anything measurable?

Possible advantages to investigate:
- accuracy/AUC
- parameter efficiency
- robustness in low-data regimes
- representation quality
- calibration

Do not define “quantum advantage” as simply beating an untuned baseline.
