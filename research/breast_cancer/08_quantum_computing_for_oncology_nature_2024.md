# Quantum Computing for Oncology

**Authors:** Siddhi Ramesh, Teague Tomesh, Samantha J. Riesenfeld, Frederic T. Chong, et al.  
**Published:** 2024, *Nature Cancer* 5, 811–816  
**Article:** https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-024-00770-9

## Why include a general oncology paper?
Your original idea also asks about **treatment/cure**, not only classification. This perspective explains where quantum computing might contribute across oncology without pretending that quantum computers currently cure cancer.

## Major opportunity areas
The article discusses potential quantum contributions to areas such as:
- molecular modelling
- genomic analysis
- medical imaging
- quantum sensing
- oncology discovery

## Connection to breast cancer
These capabilities could eventually matter for breast cancer through:
- identifying molecular subtypes
- modelling drug-target interactions
- optimizing candidate molecules
- integrating genomic/clinical information
- improving imaging/diagnostic workflows

## Key distinction
A quantum algorithm that improves molecular simulation or patient stratification is **not itself a treatment**. It could become one component of a pipeline that helps researchers discover or select treatments.

## Project implication
For a student project, diagnosis/subtyping is currently much more feasible than trying to build a “quantum cancer cure.” A drug-discovery project would normally require chemistry/biology validation beyond the quantum algorithm.

## Evidence level / caution
**Perspective/research roadmap, not clinical evidence of a quantum treatment.**
